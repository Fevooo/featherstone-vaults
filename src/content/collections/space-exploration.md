buying:
  - heading: What we look for
    body:
      - >-
        Flown status is a claim, and it is the claim that carries the value.
        We state it only where it is supported by documentation that a buyer
        can examine: programme records that identify the item, a chain of
        custody from a named participant, or a signed statement from someone
        in a position to know, supplied in original.
      - >-
        A great deal of material offered as flown is not, and a great deal of
        genuinely interesting material is not flown and does not need to be.
        Programme documents, hardware that stayed on the ground, training
        items and crew-associated material are catalogued on their own terms.
  - heading: Polar and aviation
    body:
      - >-
        Expedition material is assessed the same way. The question is whether
        the object can be tied to a named expedition or individual through
        surviving records, and whether those records can be produced.
      - >-
        Where an item came out of a family descent, we ask for the descent to
        be set out and we record it as a statement rather than as a document
        when that is what it is.
  - heading: Worth asking
    body:
      - >-
        What evidence supports the flown claim, and can it be inspected. Who
        signed any provenance letter, and what were they in a position to
        know. Is the item identifiable in programme documentation. Was it
        lawfully released.
