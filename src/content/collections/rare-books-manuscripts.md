buying:
  - heading: What we look for
    body:
      - >-
        Copies are collated against the standard bibliographies and the
        collation is reported in full, including what is wrong with it. A
        plainly described imperfect copy is a better object than a quietly
        perfected one, and a made-up copy sold as complete is the single most
        common problem in this field.
      - >-
        Supplied leaves, facsimile leaves, washing, pressing, re-margining and
        rebinding are all disclosed. Where a binding is not contemporary with
        the text we say when we think it was made.
  - heading: Condition and the record
    body:
      - >-
        Provenance in books is often better evidenced than in any other
        department, because it is written on the object: ownership
        inscriptions, armorial bookplates, shelf marks, booksellers' tickets
        and auction annotations. We record these and photograph them, and we
        resist the practice of removing them to improve appearance.
      - >-
        Maps and separately issued plates are described with their sheet
        dimensions, platemark and any trimming, and we state whether colour is
        original or later.
  - heading: Worth asking
    body:
      - >-
        Is the copy complete against the standard collation. What has been
        supplied, and from where. Has it been washed, pressed or re-margined.
        Is the binding contemporary. Is the colour original.
