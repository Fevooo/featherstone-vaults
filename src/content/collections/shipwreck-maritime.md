buying:
  - heading: What we look for
    body:
      - >-
        Recovered material is handled only where the recovery itself is
        documented and any applicable permissions, licences or salvage
        agreements can be evidenced. A piece of wreck cargo without a named
        wreck and a recovery record is decorative; with them, it is a
        historical object.
      - >-
        We ask which wreck, who carried out the recovery, under what authority,
        and in what year. Where a wreck is subject to a protection regime or a
        recovery was conducted under a government or court-supervised
        agreement, we supply what we hold.
  - heading: Conservation
    body:
      - >-
        Material raised from salt water is unstable until it has been treated.
        Ceramics and glass need desalination; iron and other metals need
        active treatment and sometimes continuing care. An untreated object
        will keep deteriorating in a drawing room.
      - >-
        The entry records what conservation has been carried out, by whom, and
        whether any continuing care is needed. Where we do not know, we say so
        and recommend an assessment before purchase.
  - heading: Worth asking
    body:
      - >-
        Which wreck, and what evidence ties this object to it. Who recovered
        it and under what permission. What conservation has been done. Does it
        need anything further.
