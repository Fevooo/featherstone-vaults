buying:
  - heading: What we look for
    body:
      - >-
        The documentary position comes before the object. We take on material
        only where ownership history can be evidenced, and the entry states the
        date at which the object can first be documented outside its country of
        origin. Where that date is later than a buyer might assume, we say so
        before an offer is made rather than afterwards.
      - >-
        Surface is the other thing we spend time on. Excavated bronze and iron
        carry a history of burial, cleaning and sometimes over-cleaning.
        Stripped, re-patinated and recut surfaces are identified in the entry.
  - heading: The legal position
    body:
      - >-
        Buyers in the United Kingdom are operating within a framework that
        includes the Dealing in Cultural Objects (Offences) Act 2003, and a
        trade convention of treating 1970, the year of the UNESCO Convention on
        cultural property, as the reference date for ownership history.
      - >-
        We do not handle material we believe to have left its country of origin
        unlawfully, and we decline objects where the position cannot be
        established. This is a commercial judgement as much as an ethical one:
        an object with an unresolved question is hard to insure, lend, move
        across a border or sell again.
  - heading: Worth asking
    body:
      - >-
        When does the documented history begin, and what evidence supports each
        step. Has the object been tested, and by which laboratory. Is an export
        licence likely to be needed, and what happens to the sale if it is
        refused.
      - >-
        A dealer should be able to answer each of these in writing before you
        commit, and should volunteer the difficult ones unprompted.
