buying:
  - heading: What we look for
    body:
      - >-
        Preparation accounts for a great deal of what a specimen is worth, and
        for most of what can go wrong with it. A heavily prepared fossil in
        which the preparator has reconstructed detail that was never there
        looks better than an honest one and is worth considerably less. We ask
        who prepared a specimen, and we examine preparation under ultraviolet
        light, which shows adhesives and fills that are invisible in normal
        light.
      - >-
        Composite specimens, assembled from more than one individual, are
        common in some groups and are not always disclosed. We decline them, or
        we describe them as composite in the entry and price them accordingly.
  - heading: Locality and classification
    body:
      - >-
        A specimen with a recorded locality, horizon and collection date is a
        different object from the same specimen without them. The difference is
        permanent, because the information cannot be reconstructed later. We
        give the collection data we hold and we say plainly when it is absent
        or inferred rather than recorded.
      - >-
        Meteorites are a separate case. Classification by a recognised
        laboratory and an entry in the Meteoritical Bulletin Database are the
        baseline, and the official name and classification appear in the entry.
        An uncatalogued stone is described as uncatalogued.
  - heading: Worth asking
    body:
      - >-
        Has it been restored, and where exactly. Is the matrix original to the
        specimen or has it been added. Is the locality recorded or inferred.
        For a meteorite, is it classified, and under what name.
      - >-
        These are reasonable questions and any dealer should be able to answer
        them in writing. If an answer is not forthcoming, that is itself an
        answer.
