buying:
  - heading: What we look for
    body:
      - >-
        Original, unrefinished condition. A case that has been polished has
        lost metal and with it the crispness of its lugs and bevels, and no
        amount of later work puts that back. We prefer an honest case with
        wear to a sharp one that has been reworked, and we say which we are
        selling.
      - >-
        Dials are where most of the value and most of the deception sits.
        Redials are frequently undisclosed and are often skilled enough to pass
        a casual look. We examine dials off the movement, under magnification,
        and the entry states whether the dial, hands and lume are original.
  - heading: Movement, case and papers
    body:
      - >-
        We record movement and case numbers, photograph the movement before
        sale, and note where a movement and case do not belong together. Where
        service records, issue records or original papers survive, copies are
        supplied with the watch.
      - >-
        For military issue watches, the markings on the case back are part of
        the object's identity. We photograph them as found and do not clean
        them.
  - heading: Worth asking
    body:
      - >-
        Is the dial original or a redial. Do the movement and case numbers
        agree with one another and with the records. What has been replaced,
        including hands, crown, crystal and lume. Has the case been polished.
        Are there service records.
