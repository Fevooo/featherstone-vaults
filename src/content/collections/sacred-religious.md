buying:
  - heading: What we look for
    body:
      - >-
        Age and surface before brightness. Devotional bronze, silver and
        pewter acquire a surface over centuries that cannot be put back once it
        has been polished off, and a great deal of ecclesiastical material on
        the market has been cleaned to a shine by someone who thought they were
        improving it. We leave patina undisturbed and we buy accordingly.
      - >-
        Later alteration is the norm rather than the exception in this field.
        Objects are repaired, re-mounted, re-gilded and adapted as liturgical
        use changes. We record those changes rather than conceal them.
  - heading: How the material reaches the market
    body:
      - >-
        Much of it comes from church closures, diocesan disposals, religious
        houses and private devotional collections. Where an object has come
        through a formal disposal we ask for the paperwork and supply it with
        the object.
      - >-
        Objects of continuing religious significance are handled with that in
        mind. Where a piece would be better returned to use or to a community,
        we will say so, and occasionally that is what happens to it.
  - heading: Worth asking
    body:
      - >-
        Has the surface been stripped or re-patinated. Is the mount, shaft or
        base original to the piece. Does it come from a documented disposal.
        Has any element been replaced.
