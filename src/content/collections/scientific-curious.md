buying:
  - heading: What we look for
    body:
      - >-
        Completeness, originality of finish, and whether the instrument still
        does what it was made to do. A telescope without its eyepieces, a
        microscope without its case and accessories, or a theodolite missing
        its tripod is a partial object and is described as one.
      - >-
        Lacquer is the usual point of damage. Brass instruments were lacquered
        when made, and a great many have been stripped and re-lacquered or
        polished bright. Original lacquer, even dulled and patchy, is worth
        more than a renewed finish, and we say which an instrument has.
  - heading: Makers and marks
    body:
      - >-
        Signatures, retailers' marks and serial numbers are recorded and
        photographed. A retailer's name is not a maker's name and we do not
        conflate the two.
      - >-
        Where an instrument can be dated from its maker's working dates or
        from a serial sequence, we give the basis for the date rather than
        just the date.
  - heading: Worth asking
    body:
      - >-
        Is it complete with its case and accessories. Is the lacquer original.
        Does it function. Is the signature a maker or a retailer. What is the
        date based on.
